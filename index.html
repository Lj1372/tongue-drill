What this trains

Language is a high-speed compression layer. It labels reality before you fully contact it. Most of the time the label arrives so fast that the raw experience is skipped.

Try not naming something for even ten seconds. The brain panics. It demands a category.

This is the core drill from Chase Hughes’ experimental book Tongue: A Cognitive Hazard: create a deliberate gap between sensory input and the verbal overlay.

How to practice

1. Choose any object, sound, or sensation.
2. Refuse every name, category, and story that arises.
3. When the word appears, notice it and drop it.
4. Stay with the raw field as long as you can.

Start short. The panic is normal. With repetition the gap widens and the internal static quiets.
